<p>While pure software PM roles are increasingly being squeezed by AI, the situation in embodied intelligence is almost the complete opposite.</p>
<p>Embodied intelligence isn’t simply about “plugging a large model into hardware.” It’s a systems engineering challenge that spans the physical and digital worlds.</p>
<p>In this field, the role of the PM isn’t diminishing—it’s being forced to evolve.</p>
<h2>1. Mistakes Here Can’t Be Fixed with “the Next Version”</h2>
<p>In the software world, a mistaken requirement usually means, at worst:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Writing a few extra lines of code</p>
</li>

<li><p>Rolling back a version</p>
</li>

<li><p>Redoing an A/B test</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>But in an embodied intelligence system:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Hardware orders have already been placed</p>
</li>

<li><p>Mechanical designs are finalized</p>
</li>

<li><p>Supply chains are locked in</p>
</li>

<li><p>On-site deployment has already happened</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>A single wrong product assumption can cost six months and millions of dollars. <br>The primary responsibility of an embodied intelligence PM isn’t to drive delivery—it’s to prevent mistakes from being scaled up. <br>I felt this deeply when delivering production lines for BMW, Audi, and Tesla at Frimo: once the mechanical design is locked in and suppliers are committed, changing requirements costs months and millions.<br>This isn’t a matter of dragging a ticket in Jira.</p>
<h2>2. You’re Not Managing Requirements—You’re Managing Irreversible System Couplings</h2>
<p>Embodied intelligence PMs don’t face a list of features; they face tightly coupled system constraints:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Sensor accuracy ↔ Control algorithm complexity</p>
</li>

<li><p>Real-time requirements ↔ Model size</p>
</li>

<li><p>Power consumption ↔ Compute power ↔ Heat dissipation ↔ Cost</p>
</li>

<li><p>Mechanical tolerances ↔ Perception robustness</p>
</li>

<li><p>Safety standards ↔ Freedom of motion strategies</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>These aren’t engineering details—they’re product-level decisions.<br>In such a system, every “requirement” permanently alters the system’s degrees of freedom.<br>This isn’t writing a PRD; it’s making systemic trade-offs.</p>
<h2>3. Embodied Intelligence PMs Must Understand the “Temperament” of the Physical World</h2>
<p>I’ve seen far too many failures where the problem wasn’t the algorithm, but reality:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Uneven floors</p>
</li>

<li><p>Changing lighting conditions</p>
</li>

<li><p>Cable aging</p>
</li>

<li><p>Temperature drift</p>
</li>

<li><p>User misuse</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>The physical world doesn’t evolve according to a roadmap, and users don’t operate the product the way it was demoed.<br>A PM without real-world, on-the-ground experience will see their product crash and burn in actual deployment.</p>
<h2>4. The Rhythm Isn’t Sprints—It’s the “Physical Beat”</h2>
<p>The pace of embodied intelligence is never:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Two-week sprints</p>
</li>

<li><p>Quarterly OKRs</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>It’s dictated by reality:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Supply chain lead times</p>
</li>

<li><p>Mechanical prototyping cycles</p>
</li>

<li><p>Safety certification processes</p>
</li>

<li><p>On-site deployment windows</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>PMs manage time windows and decision sequencing, not task lists. Getting the sequence wrong is more fatal than moving slowly.</p>
<h2>5. Embodied Intelligence PMs Are Essentially “Risk Allocators”</h2>
<p>In software, risks can be rolled back. In embodied intelligence, risks must be allocated upfront.</p>
<p>The PM has to decide:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Which risks are handled by algorithms</p>
</li>

<li><p>Which are absorbed by mechanical redundancy</p>
</li>

<li><p>Which are covered by operational processes</p>
</li>

<li><p>Which must be avoided entirely through product positioning</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>This is responsibility boundary design, not engineering detail.</p>
<h2>6. There’s No Room for “Weak PMs” Here</h2>
<p>In embodied intelligence teams:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>A PM who doesn’t understand the system slows everyone down</p>
</li>

<li><p>A PM who won’t make trade-offs amplifies risk</p>
</li>

<li><p>A PM who only pushes progress leads the team into dead ends</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>In the end, there are only two possibilities:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>The PM is exceptionally strong</p>
</li>

<li><p>Or the role effectively doesn’t exist</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>This isn’t harsh—it’s reality.</p>
<h2>One Final Honest Truth</h2>
<p>An embodied intelligence PM isn’t “someone who can write a PRD.”</p>
<p>It’s someone willing to take responsibility for the future before the system even takes shape.</p>
<p>In this field:</p>
<ul>
<li><p>Engineering capability sets the floor</p>
</li>

<li><p>Product judgment determines survival</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>That’s why I’ve always believed— Embodied intelligence isn’t where PMs disappear. It’s where PMs are truly put to the test.</p>
