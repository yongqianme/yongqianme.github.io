<ol>
<li><p>Have enough capital to support not only the company’s day-to-day operations, but also the lives of the people building it. A business needs time to grow — decisions shouldn’t be driven solely by short-term cash flow pressure.</p>
</li>

<li><p>A team needs more than aligned skills; it also needs goodwill toward one another. Over the long run, outcomes are shaped not just by professional ability, but by whether people choose understanding, trust, and support when things get difficult.</p>
</li>

<li><p>Introducing aggressive investor protections or performance-based financing terms too early is rarely easy. Capital may not intend to harm a startup, but the pressure created by certain terms can quietly drain a team’s energy, distort pace, and narrow future options before the company has matured.</p>
</li>

<li><p>Think about the consequences of failure from the beginning. A company can shut down, restart, or exit with dignity. Often harder than failure itself is getting stuck — unable to raise, under business pressure, and trapped in a state where neither moving forward nor stepping away feels possible.</p>
</li>

</ol>
<p>More important than the growth curve is preserving enough room for the company to keep moving forward — or to change direction with clarity and dignity.</p>
