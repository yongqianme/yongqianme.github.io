<h2><strong>01 | The Crossing: A Tipping Point Between Old and New Orders</strong></h2>
<p>“Every 6.5 funding rounds now includes a ‘+ round’”—in the first half of 2025, China’s venture capital market recorded 591 such “plus rounds,” creating a striking footprint of the times. In hard tech fields like semiconductors and biotech, funding rounds are sprouting extra plus signs like ivy vines: A+, A++, A+++, even A+++++. This reveals the tug-of-war between tech R&amp;D and capital patience: startups seek to extend survival, while investors adopt a cautious “small steps, quick runs” model to manage risk.</p>
<p>As tech think tank AlphaAge put it, China’s VC ecosystem is at a “crossing”: the old model of resource arbitrage is fading, while a new order based on value symbiosis is accelerating. On one hand, an Invesco report shows global sovereign wealth funds are returning to Chinese tech—59% now rank it as a high-priority investment. On the other, local governments like Wuxi are launching “Invest + Grant 2.0” initiatives to de-risk early-stage tech projects and correct market failures. A quiet revolution is already underway in the tug-of-war between <strong>resource leverage and value co-creation</strong>.</p>
<h2><strong>02 | The Evolution of Resource Leverage: From Harvesting to Planting</strong></h2>
<h3><strong>Old Paradigm: Concentrated Power, Misaligned Risk</strong></h3>
<p>In the early days of China’s VC boom, the “shadow controller” model dominated: resource gatekeepers steered policy-driven industries while young entrepreneurs charged ahead—only to see the lion’s share of returns flow to capital.</p>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>Luckin Coffee</strong> rode the twin-boom of mass entrepreneurship and innovation to scale rapidly. While founder Qian Zhiya became a startup celebrity, her ownership was heavily diluted by institutional players like Centurium Capital after IPO.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>NIO</strong> rose with support from state capital and Tencent, but founder William Li faced immense pressure from aggressive performance-based agreements.</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>This model relied on a <strong>triple leverage formula</strong>:</p>
<ol>
<li><p><strong>Policy arbitrage</strong> (e.g., new energy subsidies),</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Capital binding</strong> (preferred shares and liquidation rights),</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Tech outsourcing</strong> (R&amp;D teams lacked control).</p>
</li>

</ol>
<p>The result? Homogenized innovation: Luckin cloned Starbucks; NIO benchmarked Tesla. Disruptive originality was rare.</p>
<h3><strong>New Shape: Anchored State Capital and Embedded Ecosystems</strong></h3>
<p>With hard tech sectors gaining traction, resource leverage is being reengineered:</p>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>State capital is emerging as “patient capital”</strong>: Government-guided VC funds, through fund-of-funds, direct equity, and co-investment models, have mobilized trillions of yuan into fields like semiconductors and quantum tech.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Localized co-creation is replacing top-down directives</strong>: Firms like Yida Capital in Kunshan operate with a 10-person local team, partnering with public platforms to invest in 29 tech ventures. This has drawn corporate HQs to relocate to Kunshan, forming embedded industrial ecosystems.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Invest-Grant hybrids de-risk early-stage innovation</strong>: Institutes like Wuxi Industrial Research turn R&amp;D grants into equity, backing startups like Weishi Power, with government funds absorbing early risk and market funds scaling production.</p>
</li>

</ul>
<blockquote><p>Resource leverage hasn’t vanished—it’s just pivoted from <strong>control</strong> to <strong>co-creation</strong>.</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><strong>03 | Rise of Value Co-Creation: Tech Sovereignty Tips the Power Balance</strong></h2>
<p>While old-school power players cling to legacy models, a new generation of entrepreneurs is redrawing the rules—with <strong>technological moats as spears and contractual design as shields</strong>.</p>
<h3><strong>Tech Empowerment: From Pawns to Players</strong></h3>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>DJI</strong> commands 80% of global drone patents and retains full voting control through a dual-class (AB-share) structure. Founder Frank Wang maintains absolute decision-making power.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>NIO rewrote its performance agreement with Hefei state capital</strong>, shifting from a near-impossible revenue target (¥120B by 2024) to more sustainable R&amp;D and supply-chain impact benchmarks—moving from betting on profit to betting on ecosystem contribution.</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>In 2025, AI robotics funding in China surpassed ¥23.2B, flowing into foundational infrastructure and embodied intelligence. <strong>StarVision Technology</strong> raised $100M in an A+++ round thanks to a robust multi-modal perception algorithm—its technical moat sealed the deal.</p>
<h3><strong>Capital’s Evolution: From Catchers to Collaborators</strong></h3>
<p>VCs themselves are transforming:</p>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>Golden Tripod Capital</strong> now mandates a “three-dimensional defense” system: AB-share structures, patent pools for control, and value anchors like NIO’s battery-swap patents.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Corporate venture capital (CVC)</strong> is squeezing out traditional financial VCs: firms like Huawei and CATL provide orders and R&amp;D support, embedding portfolio startups directly into supply chains.</p>
</li>

</ul>
<blockquote><p>“VCs are no longer chess players, and founders are no longer pawns,” said Yida’s Huang Tao of the Kunshan model.<br>“We’re more like LEGO bricks—startups bring the innovation modules, and capital provides the connectors.”</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><strong>04 | Repricing China: A Global Recalibration of Assets</strong></h2>
<p>As Trump-era tariffs shook global trade, Chinese tech became a contrarian bet for sovereign wealth funds. Invesco found that <strong>funds managing $27 trillion</strong> are now calling China “the next Silicon Valley.” North American funds in particular have increased China exposure by 73%. Why?</p>
<ol>
<li><p><strong>Valuation arbitrage</strong>: Goldman Sachs’ “China Private Tech 10” (Tencent, Alibaba, Xiaomi, etc.) trade at an average P/E of 13.9—43% lower than their U.S. counterparts.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Tech breakthroughs</strong>: DeepSeek’s ultra-low-cost AI model and Alibaba’s open-source ecosystem are reshaping global expectations.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Industrial depth</strong>: Gulf state funds have declared that “China will dominate solar, EV, and battery markets.”</p>
</li>

</ol>
<p>Meanwhile, global investors are reducing exposure to USD assets—divesting U.S. Treasuries and increasing gold reserves to hedge sanctions risk. <strong>Capital is voting with real money, redrawing the global innovation map.</strong></p>
<h2><strong>05 | Entrepreneur's Playbook: Three Dimensions of Defense and Ecosystem Positioning</strong></h2>
<p>At this pivotal crossing, entrepreneurs must wield both <strong>a sharp spear of technology</strong> and <strong>a flexible shield of contracts</strong>:</p>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>Control defense</strong>: Follow DJI’s lead—AB-shares + patent pools. Avoid Luckin-style over-delegation of voting power.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Value anchor</strong>: In hard tech, secure IP (e.g., NIO’s battery swap); in consumer sectors, build supply chain dominance like Heytea.</p>
</li>

<li>
<p><strong>Resource leverage strategy</strong>:</p>

<ul>
<li><p>Deeply integrate with empowering capital</p>
</li>

<li><p>Limit dilution from controlling capital (set a 15% cap)</p>
</li>

</ul>

</li>

</ul>
<p>And above all—<strong>know your place in the ecosystem</strong>:</p>
<blockquote><p>“In Wuxi’s Invest+Grant 2.0, the soft robotics startup used public funds for prototyping, and industrial capital for mass production—<strong>find the refueling stations if you want to finish the innovation marathon.</strong>”</p>
</blockquote>
<h2><strong>06 | Conclusion: Navigating as the Tides Turn</strong></h2>
<p>China’s 2025 venture landscape is pivoting from a <strong>harvest mindset to a planting mindset</strong>. As state-guided funds, sovereign investors, and local capital converge, they are carving out a new riverbed:</p>
<ul>
<li><p><strong>Resource leverage</strong> is not obsolete—it’s just shifting from dominance to enablement.</p>
</li>

<li><p><strong>Value co-creation</strong> isn’t idealism—it’s the survival baseline rooted in tech sovereignty and robust contracts.</p>
</li>

</ul>
<p>From the bold experimentation in Kunshan to DeepSeek’s algorithmic cost revolution, one truth is emerging:</p>
<blockquote>
<p><strong>The smartest game is turning your opponent into fertile soil—so you can take root, not be buried.</strong></p>

<p>When the waters grow rough, your compass matters most.<br><strong>Clutch your technological sovereignty. A new land is rising.</strong></p>

</blockquote>
