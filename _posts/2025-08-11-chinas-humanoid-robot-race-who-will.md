<p>China’s humanoid robotics boom is real. In just two years, over 100 companies have entered the field—designing everything from factory-floor assistants to service robots for retail and healthcare.</p>
<p>But there’s a hard truth:</p>
<p>China is the world-class at building the “body” — frames, actuators, sensors, and power systems — and we can do it 30–35% cheaper than Japan or Europe.</p>
<p>The “brain” is another story — advanced AI chips are still largely foreign-made, and export restrictions are biting hard.</p>
<p>That means many companies could slip back into being hardware-only suppliers. The sector will likely see a brutal shakeout in the next 3–5 years:</p>
<p>- Most will pivot to hardware platforms, waiting for domestic chip breakthroughs.</p>
<p>- A few may pull a “DJI” — integrating body + brain and winning globally.</p>
<p>- Some might end up in state-backed consolidation or even military supply chains.</p>
<p>We’ve seen this movie before — drones, EV batteries, solar panels. The pattern repeats: rapid expansion → intense competition → consolidation → national champions.</p>
<p>The next five years will decide whether China’s humanoid robot dreams produce the next global tech giant.</p>
