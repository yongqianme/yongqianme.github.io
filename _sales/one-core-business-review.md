# One core business: review and execution alignment

Reviewed September 14, 2026 against `_plans/3m-usd-plan-2026-10-to-2027-09.md`.

## Decision

The core business is **Physical AI Deployment & Production Recovery**. Sell bounded engineering programs for valuable robot workflows with customer acceptance and reliable operation as the outcome. Start with warm industrial relationships and the existing core customer. Humanoid projects qualify when they have a real customer workflow, inspectable capability, a budget owner, and a feasible deployment intervention.

## Founder and investor review

- A founder needs a concrete delivery outcome, scope, accountable roles, and acceptance conditions. The site now explains these through diagnostic, recovery, and support stages.
- An investor needs a repeatable economic model and evidence that it can be delivered without consuming all founder time. The three stages belong to one delivery business; qualified partners must be contracted before capacity is promised.
- Product workshops, standalone advisory calls, hardware sales, and broad diligence created competing commercial paths. They have been removed from the main offer. Diligence can use the diagnostic method selectively, with commercial relationships disclosed.
- Existing experience supports technical credibility. It does not establish current delivery capacity, customer demand, accepted prices, or repeatable margins. Obtain permissioned customer results before publishing performance claims.

## Connection to the annual plan

The target remains $3M personal after-tax cash during October 2026–September 2027. The existing plan's conditional stretch assumes 20 diagnostics at $30K, 24 programs at $400K, and 18 support blocks at $100K: $12M earned and collected revenue. Its illustrative cost, tax, ownership, and retained-cash assumptions produce $3.01M personal after-tax cash. This is a scenario, not a forecast; the website does not validate those assumptions.

The implementation programs generate $9.6M of that revenue. Accordingly, the core commercial engine is delivery, not standalone two-week advice. Preserve direct entry for customers with equivalent evidence: the plan assumes eight programs following diagnostics and sixteen direct or partner programs. Support is additional work after handover; avoid counting the same scope twice.

## First 90 days

1. Validate one funded expansion with the core customer and request a permissioned case study and two introductions.
2. Qualify named sponsors, accessible operating evidence, a decision within 90 days, budget, and measurable economic impact.
3. Test the $30K / $400K / $100K price hypotheses against actual scopes and buyer acceptance. Reduce scope rather than hide delivery losses.
4. Confirm delivery leads and specialist quotes before selling implementation capacity. Keep founder time concentrated on sales and technical direction.
5. Track qualified program value, signed work, actual delivery margin, earned revenue, and collected cash separately. Replace the plan's funnel assumptions with observed results monthly.

The public site explains the customer offer. The personal cash target and internal margin assumptions stay in planning documents.
